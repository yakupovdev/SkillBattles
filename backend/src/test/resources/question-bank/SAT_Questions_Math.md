### Question 1 (ID: ac472881)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Algebra
- **Skill:** Linear equations in one variable

#### Question
$$\frac{12x + 28}{4} - \frac{s}{13} = r(x - 8)$$

In the given equation, $s$ and $r$ are constants, and $s > 0$. If the equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $s$?

**Correct Answer:** **403**

#### Rationale
The correct answer is 403. For a linear equation in one variable to have infinitely many solutions, the coefficients of the variable must be equal on both sides of the equation and the constant terms must also be equal on both sides of the equation. The given equation can be rewritten as:
$$\frac{4(3x + 7)}{4} - \frac{s}{13} = r(x - 8)$$
or
$$3x + 7 - \frac{s}{13} = r(x - 8)$$
Applying the distributive property to the right-hand side of this equation yields:
$$3x + 7 - \frac{s}{13} = rx - 8r$$
For this equation to have infinitely many solutions, the coefficients of $x$ must be equal, so it follows that $3 = r$. Additionally, the constant terms must be equal, which means:
$$7 - \frac{s}{13} = -8r$$
Substituting $3$ for $r$ in this equation yields:
$$7 - \frac{s}{13} = -8(3) \implies 7 - \frac{s}{13} = -24$$
Adding $\frac{s}{13}$ to both sides yields:
$$7 = -24 + \frac{s}{13}$$
Adding $24$ to both sides yields:
$$31 = \frac{s}{13}$$
Multiplying both sides of this equation by $13$ yields:
$$403 = s$$
Therefore, if the equation has infinitely many solutions, the value of $s$ is 403.

### Question 2 (ID: 3f5a3602)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Algebra
- **Skill:** Systems of two linear equations in two variables

#### Question
What system of linear equations is represented by the lines shown in the $xy$-plane?
*(The graph shows two lines: one passing through $(8, 0)$ and $(3, 4)$, and the other passing through $(8, 0)$ and $(3, 2)$).*

#### Choices
- **A.** $8x + 4y = 32$, $-10x - 4y = -64$
- **B.** $8x - 4y = 32$, $-10x + 4y = -64$
- **C.** $4x - 10y = 32$, $-8x + 10y = -64$
- **D.** $4x + 10y = 32$, $-8x - 10y = -64$

**Correct Answer:** **D**

#### Rationale
Choice D is correct. A line in the $xy$-plane that passes through the points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ has slope $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, and can be defined by an equation of the form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.

One of the lines shown in the graph passes through the points $(8, 0)$ and $(3, 4)$. Substituting $8$ for $x_1$, $0$ for $y_1$, $3$ for $x_2$, and $4$ for $y_2$ yields $m = \frac{4 - 0}{3 - 8} = -\frac{4}{5}$.
Substituting $-\frac{4}{5}$ for $m$, $8$ for $x_1$, and $0$ for $y_1$ yields $y - 0 = -\frac{4}{5}(x - 8)$, which is equivalent to $y = -\frac{4}{5}x + \frac{32}{5}$. Adding $\frac{4}{5}x$ to both sides yields $\frac{4}{5}x + y = \frac{32}{5}$. Multiplying both sides by $-10$ yields $-8x - 10y = -64$.

Similarly, the other line shown in the graph passes through the points $(8, 0)$ and $(3, 2)$. Substituting $8$ for $x_1$, $0$ for $y_1$, $3$ for $x_2$, and $2$ for $y_2$ yields $m = \frac{2 - 0}{3 - 8} = -\frac{2}{5}$.
Substituting $-\frac{2}{5}$ for $m$, $8$ for $x_1$, and $0$ for $y_1$ yields $y - 0 = -\frac{2}{5}(x - 8)$, which is equivalent to $y = -\frac{2}{5}x + \frac{16}{5}$. Adding $\frac{2}{5}x$ to both sides yields $\frac{2}{5}x + y = \frac{16}{5}$. Multiplying both sides by $10$ yields $4x + 10y = 32$.

So, the system of linear equations represented by the lines shown is $4x + 10y = 32$ and $-8x - 10y = -64$.

Choices A, B, and C are incorrect and may result from conceptual or calculation errors.

### Question 3 (ID: edc1b7b7)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Algebra
- **Skill:** Systems of two linear equations in two variables

#### Question
$$2(8x) + 4(7y) = 12$$
$$-2(8x) + 4(7y) = 12$$

The solution to the given system of equations is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $8x + 7y$?

**Correct Answer:** **3**

#### Rationale
The correct answer is 3. Adding the second equation to the first equation in the given system of equations yields:
$$(2(8x) - 2(8x)) + (4(7y) + 4(7y)) = 12 + 12$$
$$8(7y) = 24$$
Dividing both sides of this equation by $8$ yields $7y = 3$.
Substituting $3$ for $7y$ in the first equation, $2(8x) + 4(7y) = 12$, yields:
$$2(8x) + 4(3) = 12 \implies 2(8x) + 12 = 12$$
Subtracting $12$ from both sides yields $2(8x) = 0$, so $8x = 0$.
Substituting $0$ for $8x$ and $3$ for $7y$ in the expression $8x + 7y$ yields $0 + 3 = 3$.
Therefore, the value of $8x + 7y$ is 3.

### Question 4 (ID: 3008cfc3)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Algebra
- **Skill:** Linear equations in two variables

#### Question
| $x$ | $y$ |
| :---: | :---: |
| $k$ | $13$ |
| $k + 7$ | $-15$ |

The table gives the coordinates of two points on a line in the $xy$-plane. The $y$-intercept of the line is $(k - 5, b)$, where $k$ and $b$ are constants. What is the value of $b$?

**Correct Answer:** **33**

#### Rationale
The correct answer is 33. It’s given in the table that the coordinates of two points on a line in the $xy$-plane are $(k, 13)$ and $(k + 7, -15)$. The slope $m$ of the line is:
$$m = \frac{-15 - 13}{(k + 7) - k} = \frac{-28}{7} = -4$$
It's given that the $y$-intercept of the line is $(k - 5, b)$. Substituting $-4$ for $m$ and the coordinates $(k - 5, b)$ and $(k, 13)$ into the slope formula yields:
$$-4 = \frac{13 - b}{k - (k - 5)} = \frac{13 - b}{5}$$
Multiplying both sides of this equation by $5$ yields $-20 = 13 - b$.
Subtracting $13$ from both sides yields $-33 = -b$, so $b = 33$.
Therefore, the value of $b$ is 33.

### Question 5 (ID: 4becad44)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Algebra
- **Skill:** Systems of two linear equations in two variables

#### Question
$$3x = 36y - 45$$

One of the two equations in a system of linear equations is given. The system has no solution. Which equation could be the second equation in this system?

#### Choices
- **A.** $x = 4y$
- **B.** $\frac{1}{3}x = 4y$
- **C.** $x = 12y - 15$
- **D.** $\frac{1}{3}x = 12y - 15$

**Correct Answer:** **B**

#### Rationale
Choice B is correct. A system of two linear equations in two variables, $x$ and $y$, has no solution when the lines in the $xy$-plane representing the equations are parallel and distinct (same slope, different $y$-intercepts).
The given equation $3x = 36y - 45$ can be rewritten in slope-intercept form:
$$36y = 3x + 45 \implies y = \frac{3}{36}x + \frac{45}{36} = \frac{1}{12}x + \frac{5}{4}$$
The slope is $\frac{1}{12}$ and the $y$-intercept is $(0, \frac{5}{4})$.
Therefore, the second equation must have slope $\frac{1}{12}$ and a $y$-intercept other than $(0, \frac{5}{4})$.
Multiplying each side of the equation in choice B by $\frac{1}{4}$ yields $\frac{1}{12}x = y$, or $y = \frac{1}{12}x$. This line has slope $\frac{1}{12}$ and $y$-intercept $(0, 0)$. Since the slopes are equal and the $y$-intercepts are different, choice B produces a system with no solution.

Choice A has slope $\frac{1}{4}$, so it intersects the line in one point. Choice C is equivalent to the given equation (infinitely many solutions). Choice D has slope $\frac{1}{36}$, so it intersects the line in one point.

#### Question
The graph of line $h$ is shown in the $xy$-plane (passing through $(-6, 0)$ and $(0, -9)$). Line $k$ (not shown) is defined by $sx + 48y = t$, where $s$ and $t$ are constants. If line $k$ is graphed in this $xy$-plane, the result is a graph of two linear equations. This system of two linear equations has no solution. Which of the following is NOT a possible value of $t$?

#### Choices
- **A.** $-432$
- **B.** $-9$
- **C.** $72$
- **D.** $288$

**Correct Answer:** **A**

#### Rationale
Choice A is correct. Since the system has no solution, line $h$ and line $k$ must be parallel with different $y$-intercepts.
The $y$-intercept of line $h$ is $(0, -9)$.
For line $k$, substituting $x = 0$ into $sx + 48y = t$ gives $48y = t \implies y = \frac{t}{48}$, so its $y$-intercept is $(0, \frac{t}{48})$.
Since the two lines cannot have the same $y$-intercept:
$$\frac{t}{48} \neq -9 \implies t \neq -9(48) = -432$$
Thus, $-432$ is NOT a possible value of $t$.

Choice B is incorrect because $t = -9$ gives $y$-intercept $-\frac{9}{48} \neq -9$, which is possible. Choices C and D give $y$-intercepts different from $-9$, so they are possible values of $t$.

### Question 10 (ID: ff501705)

#### Question
$$\frac{3}{2}y - \frac{1}{4}x = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{3}{2}y$$
$$\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{2} = py + \frac{9}{2}$$

In the given system of equations, $p$ is a constant. If the system has no solution, what is the value of $p$?

**Correct Answer:** **6**

#### Rationale
The correct answer is 6. For a system of two linear equations to have no solution, the lines must be parallel and distinct.
Multiplying the first equation by $12$:
$$18y - 3x = 8 - 18y \implies -3x + 36y = 8$$
Multiplying the second equation by $2$:
$$x + 3 = 2py + 9 \implies x - 2py = 6$$
For the lines to be parallel, the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ must be proportional:
$$\frac{-3}{1} = \frac{36}{-2p} \implies -3 = -\frac{18}{p} \implies p = 6$$
Checking constants: $\frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3} \neq -3$, so the lines are distinct and parallel. Thus, $p = 6$.

### Question 12 (ID: 781c2f6e)

#### Question
The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = a(2.2^x + 2.2^b)$, where $a$ and $b$ are integer constants and $0 < a < b$. The functions $g$ and $h$ are equivalent to function $f$, where $k$ and $m$ are constants. Which of the following equations displays the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = f(x)$ in the $xy$-plane as a constant or coefficient?
I. $g(x) = a(2.2^x + k)$
II. $h(x) = a(2.2)^x + m$

#### Choices
- **A.** I only
- **B.** II only
- **C.** I and II
- **D.** Neither I nor II

**Correct Answer:** **D**

#### Rationale
Choice D is correct. The $y$-intercept occurs at $x = 0$:
$$f(0) = a(2.2^0 + 2.2^b) = a(1 + 2.2^b) = a + a(2.2)^b$$
For equation I: $g(x) = a(2.2^x + k)$ is equivalent to $f(x)$ if $k = 2.2^b$. But the $y$-intercept is $a + a(2.2)^b$, which does not equal $k$ or $a$, since $0 < a < b$.
For equation II: $h(x) = a(2.2)^x + m$ is equivalent to $f(x)$ if $m = a(2.2)^b$. But the $y$-intercept is $a + a(2.2)^b$, which does not equal $m$ (it is $a + m$) and does not equal $a$.
Thus, neither function displays the $y$-intercept value $a + a(2.2)^b$ as a constant or coefficient.

### Question 13 (ID: 4661e2a9)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Advanced Math
- **Skill:** Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems of equations in two variables

#### Question
$$x - y = 1$$
$$x + y = x^2 - 3$$

Which ordered pair is a solution to the system of equations above?

#### Choices
- **A.** $(1 + \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3})$
- **B.** $(\sqrt{3}, -\sqrt{3})$
- **C.** $(1 + \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5})$
- **D.** $(\sqrt{5}, -1 + \sqrt{5})$

**Correct Answer:** **A**

#### Rationale
Choice A is correct. From the first equation, $x = y + 1$. Substituting into the second equation:
$$(y + 1) + y = (y + 1)^2 - 3$$
$$2y + 1 = y^2 + 2y + 1 - 3 = y^2 + 2y - 2$$
Subtracting $2y$ from both sides:
$$1 = y^2 - 2 \implies y^2 = 3 \implies y = \pm \sqrt{3}$$
If $y = \sqrt{3}$, then $x = 1 + \sqrt{3}$. Thus, $(1 + \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3})$ is a solution.

### Question 19 (ID: 46308566)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Advanced Math
- **Skill:** Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems of equations in two variables

#### Question
$$r^2 + qr = 2r - 55$$

In the given equation, $q$ is an integer constant. The given equation has no real solutions. What is the largest possible value of $q$?

**Correct Answer:** **16**

#### Rationale
The correct answer is 16. Rearranging the equation into standard quadratic form:
$$r^2 + (q - 2)r + 55 = 0$$
A quadratic equation $ar^2 + br + c = 0$ has no real solutions if and only if its discriminant is strictly negative:
$$b^2 - 4ac < 0 \implies (q - 2)^2 - 4(1)(55) < 0$$
$$(q - 2)^2 - 220 < 0 \implies (q - 2)^2 < 220$$
$$-\sqrt{220} < q - 2 < \sqrt{220}$$
$$2 - \sqrt{220} < q < 2 + \sqrt{220}$$
Since $\sqrt{220} \approx 14.83$:
$$-12.83 < q < 16.83$$
Since $q$ is an integer, the largest possible value of $q$ is 16.

### Question 21 (ID: 983412ea)
- **Difficulty:** HARD
- **Assessment:** SAT
- **Test:** Math
- **Domain:** Geometry and Trigonometry
- **Skill:** Area and volume

#### Question
A right square prism has a height of $14\text{ units}$. The volume of the prism is $2{,}016\text{ cubic units}$. What is the length, in units, of an edge of the base?

**Correct Answer:** **12**

#### Rationale
The correct answer is 12. The volume, $V$, of a right square prism can be calculated using the formula $V = s^2h$, where $s$ represents the length of an edge of the base and $h$ represents the height of the prism. It's given that the volume of the prism is $2{,}016\text{ cubic units}$ and the height is $14\text{ units}$. Substituting $2{,}016$ for $V$ and $14$ for $h$ in the formula yields:
$$2{,}016 = (s^2)(14)$$
Dividing both sides of this equation by $14$ yields:
$$144 = s^2$$
